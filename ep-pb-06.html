EP-PB-06: Mimikatz / Credential Dumping Detection

Endpoint playbook for responding to Mimikatz or equivalent credential dumping detections, with emphasis on LSASS access validation, identity exposure assessment, lateral movement scoping, and credential recovery.

Section 1
Purpose

Provide an end-to-end response model for suspected or confirmed Mimikatz activity or equivalent credential dumping behaviour on Windows endpoints. The objective is to contain the host, determine whether credential material was exposed, protect privileged accounts, and prevent lateral movement.

Critical rule: treat any credible LSASS credential dumping event as a potential credential compromise, not only as malware execution. Do not close the incident after file quarantine alone.

Section 2
Activation triggers
  • EDR/XDR detection naming Mimikatz, credential dumping, LSASS access, credential theft, or suspicious memory scraping.
  • Process opening lsass.exe with high-risk access rights, especially from non-security tooling, user-writable paths, scripting hosts, or temporary directories.
  • Creation of LSASS dump files, suspicious use of dump utilities, or archive files staged soon after LSASS access.
  • Suspicious commands, modules, or strings associated with credential theft activity discovered in telemetry, memory, files, or command-line logs.
  • Post-detection signs of credential use: abnormal sign-ins, new sessions from the affected user, lateral movement, service creation, remote logons, or privileged group changes.

Section 4
Response workflow
1
Validate the alert. Confirm detection source, host, user, parent process, process path, hash, command line, and whether LSASS was accessed or dumped.
2
Contain the endpoint. Isolate the endpoint through EDR where business impact is acceptable; otherwise apply compensating network containment approved by Tier-2.
3
Protect identities. Identify all interactive, service, cached, and privileged accounts that may have had credential material in memory; revoke sessions and rotate credentials based on exposure risk.
4
Scope laterally. Hunt for the same hash, filename, command-line patterns, LSASS access pattern, remote logons, service creation, WMI/WinRM/RDP use, and authentication anomalies across the estate.
5
Eradicate and recover. Remove artefacts only after evidence is captured. Rebuild rather than clean if privileged credentials, domain admin sessions, or unknown persistence are involved.
6
Close with hardening actions. Confirm LSASS protection, Credential Guard / LSA protection where applicable, ASR rule coverage, privileged access hygiene, and monitoring detections.

Section 7
Closure criteria
  • No active credential dumping artefacts remain on affected systems.
  • All exposed accounts have been assessed and reset/rotated according to risk.
  • No related lateral movement, remote execution, or anomalous authentication remains unresolved.
  • Endpoint has been rebuilt or cleaned with Tier-2 approval and monitoring has confirmed stability.
  • Lessons learned and control gaps have been logged in the incident record.
