EP-WI-05: Validate LSASS Credential Dumping Indicators

Tool-agnostic investigation work instruction for validating Mimikatz, LSASS access, and credential dumping indicators without executing credential theft tools or exposing secrets.

Section 1
Purpose

Provide a tool-agnostic checklist for validating LSASS credential dumping indicators associated with Mimikatz or equivalent tooling. This WI supports evidence collection and decision-making; it does not authorise offensive testing or live credential dumping.

Scope boundary: do not run credential dumping tools, do not open suspicious dumps on production systems, and do not attempt to recover passwords. Preserve artefacts and follow approved forensic handling.

Section 2
When to use
  • EDR alert mentions Mimikatz, credential dumping, LSASS access, or credential theft.
  • A process accesses lsass.exe unexpectedly or with high-risk access rights.
  • Dump files, suspicious archives, or credential-theft strings are found during endpoint triage.
  • EP-RB-05 requires analyst validation of process, file, identity, and lateral movement evidence.
Section 3
Validation procedure
1
Capture alert context. Record alert ID, source platform, detection name, host, user, process, hash, file path, timestamp, and current containment status.
2
Review process tree. Identify the parent process, grandparent process, command-line arguments, working directory, signer, reputation, and whether the path is user-writable.
3
Validate LSASS access. Check whether the process opened or attempted to open lsass.exe, what access level was requested, whether the action was blocked, and whether the actor had administrative rights.
4
Search for dump artefacts. Look for recent dump files, large temporary files, renamed archive files, memory dump naming patterns, and file creation activity immediately after LSASS access.
5
Assess account exposure. List users with active or recent interactive sessions on the endpoint, prioritising administrators, service accounts, privileged accounts, and remote support users.
6
Check follow-on activity. Review for remote logons, SMB/RDP/WinRM/WMI usage, suspicious service creation, scheduled tasks, abnormal authentication, or new network connections after the detection time.
7
Document verdict. Classify as false positive, blocked attempt, suspected credential exposure, or confirmed credential exposure; attach evidence to the incident ticket.

Section 6
Escalation triggers
  • LSASS dump was created or likely created.
  • Any privileged account had an active session on the host.
  • The affected host is a server, PAW, jump host, domain controller, backup server, or admin workstation.
  • Evidence of pass-the-hash, lateral movement, remote execution, or abnormal sign-ins after the event.
  • Security controls were disabled, tampered with, or bypassed.
Section 7
Expected outcome

The analyst produces a documented verdict and evidence pack sufficient for Tier-2 to decide whether to activate EP-PB-06, trigger identity recovery, rebuild the endpoint, or close as false positive / blocked attempt.
