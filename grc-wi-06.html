GRC-WI-06: Translate Technical Risk into Business Risk

Step-by-step work instruction for converting technical findings, incidents, vulnerabilities, and control gaps into clear business risk statements.

Work instructionBusiness languageRisk statementsEscalation
Document ID: GRC-WI-06| Type: Work Instruction| Status: Approved| Version: v1.0| Owner: GRC Owner| Last updated: 3 Sep 2026
Purpose
When to use this work instruction

Use this work instruction when a technical issue needs to be converted into a risk record, governance update, management report, risk acceptance request, or enterprise risk escalation. It is intended for GRC analysts, SOC leads, IT operations owners, and risk reviewers.

Inputs
Information required before translation
InputWhy it matters
Technical finding or eventDefines what was observed: vulnerability, alert, audit finding, control failure, incident, supplier gap, or architecture weakness.
Affected asset/service/dataIdentifies what could be harmed and who depends on it.
Threat or failure scenarioExplains how the weakness could realistically be exploited or fail.
Existing controlsShows whether likelihood or impact is already reduced.
Business dependencyConnects the technical issue to operations, compliance, service delivery, finance, reputation, or resilience.
Procedure
Translation steps
1
Describe the technical condition. Capture the observable issue in plain language without overstating impact.
2
Identify the affected business object. Map the issue to service, process, data, user group, supplier, application, location, or critical asset.
3
Define the credible scenario. State what could happen if the issue is exploited, fails, or remains untreated.
4
Express business impact. Translate the scenario into operational, financial, legal, reputational, safety, resilience, or strategic impact.
5
Assess control confidence. Record preventive, detective, corrective, and compensating controls, including evidence quality.
6
Draft the risk statement. Use the standard pattern: There is a risk that [event] could occur because of [cause], resulting in [impact] affecting [asset/service/data/process].
7
Assign ownership and decision need. Identify who can accept, treat, transfer, or avoid the risk and what decision is required.
Examples
Technical-to-business translation examples
Technical findingWeak wordingBetter risk wording
Critical vulnerability on internet-facing serverThe server has a critical CVE.There is a risk that an attacker could compromise the public service because a known critical vulnerability remains unremediated, resulting in service disruption, data exposure, and emergency recovery activity.
Shared admin accountThere is a shared admin account.There is a risk that privileged activity cannot be attributed to an individual because shared administrative credentials are in use, reducing accountability and increasing the impact of credential compromise.
Backups not testedBackups are not being tested.There is a risk that critical services cannot be restored within business tolerance because backup restoration has not been validated, resulting in extended outage during a major incident.
Supplier lacks MFASupplier does not use MFA.There is a risk that supplier access could be compromised because strong authentication is not enforced, resulting in unauthorised access to supported systems or sensitive data.
Quality checks
Before publishing or escalating a risk

Business consequence is clear

A non-technical stakeholder can understand why the risk matters.

Cause is not confused with impact

The vulnerability, gap, or finding explains the cause; the consequence explains the risk.

Owner can act

The named owner has authority or influence over treatment or acceptance.

Decision is explicit

The record says whether mitigation, acceptance, transfer, avoidance, or further analysis is needed.
