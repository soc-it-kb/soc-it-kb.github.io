Cyber & IT Risk Appetite Statement

Provides a generic cyber and IT risk appetite model that can be adapted by organisations to define acceptable, tolerable, and unacceptable levels of risk.

Purpose
How to use this statement

This page provides a generic risk appetite model for cyber and IT risk. It should be adapted to the organisation’s sector, obligations, funding model, regulatory environment, operating maturity, and critical service profile.

Important: risk appetite is not permission to ignore risk. It defines the level of residual risk leadership is willing to operate within, and the thresholds where escalation, treatment, or formal acceptance is required.

Default position
Overall cyber and IT risk appetite
Risk areaDefault appetiteInterpretation
Critical services and resilienceLowCritical services should not depend on untested recovery, unsupported infrastructure, or single points of failure without documented acceptance.
Privileged access and identityLowStanding privilege, weak authentication, orphaned accounts, and unmanaged service accounts should be minimised and actively reviewed.
Sensitive and regulated dataLowExposure of sensitive data should be prevented through classification, access control, encryption, monitoring, and supplier assurance.
Internet-facing systemsLow to ModerateExposure is acceptable only when systems are known, owned, patched, monitored, and protected by appropriate controls.
Standard operational technology riskModerateSome residual risk may be tolerated where impact is limited, controls are operating, and remediation is tracked.
Innovation and experimentationModerateControlled experimentation is acceptable when data, identity, logging, and segregation controls are in place.

TriggerEscalation expectation

Sensitive data exposure riskEscalate where regulated, confidential, or high-volume data may be exposed.
Repeated policy or baseline exceptionsEscalate as a systemic control weakness, not isolated exceptions.
Supplier risk affecting critical serviceEscalate when supplier weakness could disrupt critical operations or expose sensitive data.
BCP/DR gap for critical serviceEscalate where recovery capability has not been tested or does not meet business tolerance.
Acceptance rules
Minimum rules for accepted risk

Named accountable owner

Accepted risk must be owned by someone with business or service accountability.

Time-bound acceptance

Acceptance must expire and be reviewed; indefinite acceptance should not be permitted.

Compensating controls

Document what reduces likelihood, impact, or detection/response time while the risk remains open.

Evidence and rationale

Record the reason for acceptance, options considered, and why treatment is not currently pursued.
