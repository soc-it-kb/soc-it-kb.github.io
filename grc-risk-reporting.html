Cyber Risk Reporting Model

Defines a practical reporting model for communicating cyber and IT risk posture to operational teams, management, governance forums, and executive stakeholders.

Risk reportingKRIsGovernance forumsExecutive visibility
Document ID: GRC-RISK-REP-01| Type: Guidance| Status: Approved| Version: v1.0| Owner: GRC Owner| Last updated: 3 Sep 2026
Purpose
Why risk reporting matters

Risk reporting converts risk records into decisions. The purpose is not to produce more dashboards, but to show whether risk is increasing or decreasing, whether treatment is effective, whether risk remains within appetite, and where leadership action is required.

Audience model
Different audiences need different risk views
AudiencePrimary questionReporting focusFrequency
SOC and IT operationsWhat needs action now?New risks, urgent vulnerabilities, failed controls, overdue remediation, incidents driving risk changes.Daily to weekly
GRC and risk ownersAre risks being managed according to process?Risk register quality, treatment plans, accepted risk expiry, control gaps, evidence completeness.Weekly to monthly
Management forumWhere do we need prioritisation or resource decisions?Top risks, risks above appetite, overdue actions, recurring exceptions, cross-domain dependencies.Monthly
Executive / board-level stakeholdersWhat could materially affect objectives, resilience, compliance, reputation, or service delivery?Enterprise-level risk themes, risk movement, material decisions, assurance confidence, major residual exposures.Quarterly or governance cadence

Reporting structure
Recommended monthly cyber risk report
SectionContent
Executive summaryOne-page view of risk posture, key changes, decisions required, and risks above appetite.
Risk movementNew, closed, increased, decreased, accepted, and overdue risks since the last report.
Top risksTop 5–10 risks by residual rating, business impact, trend, and decision need.
Treatment statusMitigation plans, overdue actions, blockers, funding or ownership issues.
Control assuranceKey control indicators, failed control tests, evidence gaps, and compensating controls.
Exceptions and acceptancesOpen exceptions, expiring acceptances, repeated patterns, and risk appetite breaches.
Forward lookUpcoming reviews, threat trends, audit activity, major changes, and risk themes to watch.
Escalation rules
When reporting must trigger action

Risk above appetite

High and Critical residual risks require named action or formal acceptance.

Negative trend

A deteriorating risk trend should trigger review even before thresholds are breached.

Control failure

Failed key controls should be linked to affected risks and remediation owners.

Repeated exception

Recurring exceptions should be reported as systemic risk, not isolated operational deviations.

Recommended evidence sources
SourceUse in reporting

Risk Acceptance LogAccepted residual risks, expiry dates, compensating controls, and accountable owners.
Policy and baseline exception registersExceptions that may indicate tolerance breaches or systemic control weakness.
Validation and drift registersEvidence of control effectiveness, failed checks, and recurring configuration drift.
Vulnerability and patching reportsExposure, remediation progress, SLA performance, and KEV prioritisation.
Incident and problem recordsEvents that change likelihood, impact, control confidence, or risk treatment priority.
