Cyber Risk and Enterprise Risk Management

Explains how cyber and IT risk should be positioned, escalated, and governed as part of broader enterprise risk management rather than treated as a purely technical issue.

ERM alignmentBusiness impactGovernance oversightNIST CSF Govern
Document ID: GRC-ERM-01| Type: Guidance| Status: Approved| Version: v1.0| Owner: GRC Owner| Last updated: 3 Sep 2026
Purpose
Why this page exists

Cyber and IT risks should be expressed in terms that business leaders can use to make decisions. This page provides the bridge between technical risk records, operational security findings, supplier assessments, business continuity concerns, and the enterprise risk register.

Operating principle: cyber risk is a business risk with technical causes. The GRC function should preserve technical evidence while translating exposure into business impact, decision options, ownership, and accountability.

Minimum evidence
Evidence required for enterprise escalation

Business consequence

Describe realistic operational, financial, legal, reputational, safety, or resilience impact.

Risk owner

Assign a person or role with authority to accept, fund, transfer, or remediate the risk.

Control position

State current controls, known gaps, compensating controls, and confidence level.

Decision needed

Make clear whether leadership is being asked to accept, fund, prioritise, avoid, or transfer the risk.
