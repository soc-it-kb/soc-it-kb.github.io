Cyber & IT Risk Taxonomy

Defines standard risk categories, event types, causes, impacts, and ownership language so risk records remain consistent across the knowledge base.

Risk categoriesScenario languageConsistent scoringOwnership
Document ID: GRC-RISK-TAX-01| Type: Reference| Status: Approved| Version: v1.0| Owner: GRC Owner| Last updated: 3 Sep 2026
Purpose
Why a taxonomy is required

A risk taxonomy prevents risk registers from becoming inconsistent collections of vulnerabilities, incidents, audit findings, and generic concerns. It defines the common language used to classify cyber and IT risk records, compare trends, assign ownership, and report risk posture.

Risk categories
Standard cyber and IT risk categories
CategoryScopeExample risk statement
Identity and access riskHuman identities, privileged access, service accounts, authentication, entitlement lifecycle.Privileged access is not sufficiently governed, increasing the likelihood of unauthorised access to critical systems.
Data protection riskData classification, storage, sharing, exfiltration, retention, encryption, privacy dependency.Sensitive data may be exposed because storage and sharing controls are inconsistently applied.
Cloud and SaaS riskCloud tenants, SaaS platforms, control plane access, storage, logging, configuration, shared responsibility.Cloud resources may be exposed publicly due to misconfiguration and weak preventive guardrails.
Endpoint and workload riskWorkstations, servers, workloads, EDR coverage, malware, hardening, local privilege.Unmanaged endpoints may be compromised without timely detection or containment.
Network and infrastructure riskSegmentation, firewalls, DNS, remote access, exposed services, network device baselines.Flat network segments may allow lateral movement after initial compromise.
Vulnerability and patch riskKnown vulnerabilities, KEV exposure, unsupported platforms, patch SLAs, remediation ownership.Critical vulnerabilities may remain exploitable beyond defined remediation tolerance.
Third-party and supplier riskSuppliers, outsourced support, SaaS providers, contractual controls, access and assurance.Supplier access may introduce unmanaged exposure to systems or data.
Resilience and recovery riskBCP, DR, backup, restore testing, crisis communications, service recovery dependencies.Critical services may not recover within expected business tolerances after disruption.
Compliance and legal riskRegulatory obligations, audit findings, policy non-compliance, evidence gaps.Inadequate evidence may prevent demonstration of compliance obligations.
Security operations riskDetection coverage, log retention, incident response capacity, triage quality, escalation.Insufficient monitoring coverage may delay detection and response to material incidents.
Risk statement pattern
Recommended wording structure

Use this pattern: There is a risk that [event] could occur because of [cause/control weakness], resulting in [business impact] affecting [asset/service/data/process].

ElementQuestionExample
EventWhat could happen?Unauthorised access to privileged systems.
CauseWhy could it happen?Privileged accounts are not periodically reviewed.
ImpactWhy does it matter?Operational disruption, data exposure, or regulatory breach.
Affected objectWhat is exposed?Critical business applications, cloud tenants, financial systems, or sensitive data.
Impact taxonomy
Impact types for scoring and reporting
Impact typeDefinitionExamples
OperationalEffect on service delivery, productivity, availability, or business process continuity.Service outage, manual workaround, degraded user access.
FinancialDirect or indirect financial loss, recovery cost, fraud, penalties, or lost revenue.Incident response cost, ransomware recovery, supplier remediation.
Legal and regulatoryBreach of law, contract, regulatory obligation, audit finding, or reporting requirement.Personal data breach, contractual SLA failure, audit non-conformity.
ReputationalLoss of trust among users, customers, partners, donors, regulators, or the public.Public incident disclosure, media coverage, confidence loss.
Safety and wellbeingPotential harm to people, communities, employees, or beneficiaries.Loss of access to services supporting health, education, or field operations.
StrategicEffect on strategic initiatives, transformation, funding, or long-term capability.Delayed migration, blocked programme, inability to scale operations.
Register usage
How to use the taxonomy in risk records

One primary category

Assign one primary category for reporting, even if the risk touches several domains.

Secondary tags allowed

Use secondary tags for cloud, supplier, identity, data, continuity, or regulatory context.

Avoid findings as risks

Do not record “missing patch” as the risk. Record the business risk created by delayed patching.

Review categories quarterly

Use trends by category to identify systemic control weaknesses and investment priorities.
